About

I am a linguist working on the phonology and morphology of Brazilian Sign Language (Libras). My research examines how manual and non-manual articulators share phonological features, and how this sharing contributes to the expression of grammatical meaning, especially aspectual properties of events. A recurring concern in my work is the need to describe sign languages on their own terms, taking into account articulatory simultaneity, the three-dimensional signing space and the linguistic role of the face, head and body, rather than transposing categories developed for spoken languages.

I teach in the Libras Teacher Education program (Licenciatura em Letras-Libras) and in the Graduate Program in Linguistics at UFJF, where I coordinate FONOLIS, the Research Group on Phonology and Interfaces in Sign Languages. I hold a PhD and an MA in Linguistics from the Pontifical Catholic University of Minas Gerais (PUC Minas). I am a hearing signer, fluent in Libras since childhood and in International Sign, and hold national certification (Prolibras) in both Libras teaching and Libras–Portuguese interpreting.

Research

Non-manual phonology

Phonological features of non-manual articulators and their temporal alignment with manual activity, modeled within the Prosodic Model of sign language phonology.

Aspect and event structure

How telicity, duration and other aspectual properties of eventualities are expressed compositionally by manual and non-manual features in Libras verbs.

Morphology and word formation

Noun–verb derivation, categorizing features and lexical expansion in Libras, including neologisms emerging in academic contexts.

Variation and usage

Phonetic and phonological variation in fingerspelling and signing, approached from a variationist perspective.

Research group

FONOLIS · Phonology and Interfaces in Sign Languages (UFJF, registered with CNPq). The group brings together undergraduate and graduate researchers working on the phonetics, phonology and morphology of Libras, with corpus annotation in ELAN and facial action analysis with FaceReader. Follow our activities on Instagram: @fonoliscnpq.

I am also a member of GREMD, the Research Group on Distributed Morphology at the University of São Paulo (USP).

Teaching

Graduate courses at UFJF include Phonetics and Phonology (2026) and Modality, Grammar and Visuospatiality in Linguistic Structure (2025). At the undergraduate level I teach Libras linguistics in the Letras-Libras program, including Libras phonology and sociolinguistic variation. I also coordinate the specialization program in Deaf Education and Libras Teaching.
